# Venue landscape for NFL touchdown props (US, fall 2026): pick'em apps vs sportsbooks vs sharp books vs prediction markets

Research date: 2026-10-17. Reader profile: US recreational bettor placing ~3 entries x $20 per NFL weekend, mostly 2-3 leg touchdown parlays. All "hold" and "cost per $20" numbers marked *(my arithmetic)* are computed from the cited payout tables / fee formulas, not quoted from the source. Primary-source pages that could not be fetched (Kalshi fee-schedule PDF returned HTTP 429; Underdog/PrizePicks help-center pages and Legal Sports Report returned 403) are flagged; where I relied on a search-engine snippet of such a page, that is flagged too.

## Key Question 1: Which venue charges the least for a 2-3 leg touchdown parlay, after all fees, for a small bettor?

### Takeaway
On documented fees alone, the exchange-style venues (ProphetX 0% on parlays; Kalshi Combos at ≤1.75¢/contract taker fee; Novig parlays with a 0.10×P×(1−P) fee built into the quote) and a sportsbook cross-game parlay with ~-110 legs (~9% hold on 2 legs) are far cheaper than fixed-table pick'em entries (2-pick 3x = 25% hold on fair legs; 3-pick 5x = 37.5%), with Underdog's 2026 table (3.5x / 6.5x = 12.5% / 18.75%) the cheapest pick'em. The ranking among the exchanges and sportsbooks is NOT settled, because no public dataset measures the spread between Kalshi/Novig/ProphetX parlay quotes and the product of fair leg prices; the best-documented cheap exposure to TD props is Kalshi anytime-TD singles (1¢ median spread, no maker fee), which is a different product from a parlay.

#### B. Regulated sportsbooks: TD-market pricing and parlay construction
- Cross-game parlay price = product of leg odds; SGP price is correlation-adjusted by proprietary models. Example (prices Oct 13, 2026): Dak 277+ yds + Lamb 77+ yds — multiplying DK's leg prices gives 28.0% (+257); DK's SGP quote was +166 (37.6%), i.e., "scaled that up by about a third"; the simulator's joint probability was 28.9% (fair +245). All nine DK/FD SGPs tested paid below the simulated fair price, with average loss per $100 from $6 (DK +170 vs fair +188) to $39 (DK +370 vs fair +667), versus ~$4.50 per $100 for a standard -110 bet. FanDuel paid less than DraftKings on identical legs (+321 vs +350; +154 vs +166). — [OddsShopper, "How are SGP odds calculated," Oct 14, 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/how-same-game-parlay-odds-are-calculated)
- Two-way "2+ passing TD" (over/under 1.5) sportsbook prices, Oct 15, 2026 (book not named): Darnold +130/-175; Nix +180/-250. *(My arithmetic: both pairs sum to 107.1% implied, i.e., ~7.1% hold.)* Same article quotes "Darnold under 218.5 pass yds -115 at BetMGM" and "Smith-Njigba over 6.5 receptions -127 at Novig." — [SportsBettingDime TNF props, Oct 15, 2026](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/nfl/seahawks-vs-broncos-player-props-picks-lines-tnf-oct-15/)
- Anytime-TD markets at US books are mostly one-way ("Yes" only). An older Action Network single-game snapshot (re-reported Oct 11, 2026; figures "several seasons old") showed SuperBook at 4.1% implied hold when it posted Yes/No, versus 28.9% implied board-level hold for its Yes-only board, with other Yes-only books at roughly 27%-47% (board-level, assumption-driven estimate, not a per-bet cost). — [The Lock Talk, Oct 11, 2026](https://thelocktalk.substack.com/p/youre-backing-the-wrong-running-back); original: [Action Network TD props study (~2021)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/best-sportsbooks-first-touchdown-anytime-td-props)
- A UK-oriented guide reports TD-scorer overrounds of 15-20% vs 5-8% on match betting (single writer's observation). — [nfltouchdownbets.com](https://nfltouchdownbets.com/)
- General player-prop holds: mainstream game lines 4-5% vs player props 7-12% at top US books; "No US regulator breaks props out as a category." *(My arithmetic: -115/-115 = 6.98% hold; -120/-120 = 9.09%.)* — [sportsbooks.ag prop-hold explainer](https://sportsbooks.ag/reddit/best-prop-betting-site-reddit/)

#### C. Prediction markets / exchanges: fee schedules (what a $20 ticket actually costs)
- Kalshi taker fee = M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P), maker fee = M × 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P), rounded up; default maker multiplier "listed as zero unless otherwise indicated"; pro and college football series have taker multiplier 1 and "several sports series show a maker multiplier of 1"; no settlement or membership fee; ACH free, card deposits up to 2%; schedule effective July 7, 2026. $100 taker trade costs ≈$3.50 at 50¢, ≈$2.80 at 60¢, ≈$4.20 at 40¢, ≈$6.30 at 10¢. — [TrueBet, Aug 19, 2026](https://blog.truebet.app/2026/08/kalshi-fees-explained-what-a-100-sports-trade-really-costs/) (citing the [Kalshi fee schedule PDF](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf), which returned HTTP 429 when fetched directly)
- Kalshi help center: "Kalshi makes money by charging a transaction fee on the expected earnings on the contract"; only some markets have maker fees; maker fees are charged only when a resting order executes; no fee to cancel. — [Kalshi help: Fees (Apr 19, 2026)](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823805-fees). Note: the "12 bps taker / 5 bps maker" tiers surfaced in search belong to Kalshi's margin/perpetuals fee-tier API, not event contracts. — [Kalshi margin fee tiers API](https://docs.kalshi.com/margin-rest/fees/get-fee-tiers)
- Kalshi Combos (parlays): priced by Request-for-Quote; "If no market participant responds to the RFQ with a quote, the combo order will not fill"; quoted price can change before execution; combo payout = product of leg settlement values, $0 if any leg settles at $0; DNP legs settle at "last fair price," not void; no combo-specific fee stated. — [Kalshi help: Combos](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823820-combos). Same-game legs are allowed ("player props and game lines to create same-game Combos"); each Combo is its own market with a $1 max settlement per contract. — [SI Kalshi NFL review, Oct 16, 2026](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi-nfl); [WTOP Kalshi Combos guide](https://wtop.com/prediction-markets/kalshi-combos/)
- Kalshi changelog (fees on combos): Sept 10, 2026 — target-cost RFQs may opt out of "fee-inclusive sizing," so "taker fees charged on top instead of reserved inside the cash amount" (i.e., RFQ/combo orders do carry the taker fee by default). Aug 20, 2026 — combos "composed entirely of independent NFL components will have no maker fee" (independent = every component tied to a different NFL game); maker fees on combo RFQs enabled 5:00 AM ET Aug 20, 2026; if a quoter executes against an order resting <5 seconds, fees flip and the quoter's maker fee uses "a fee multiplier of 0.5, rather than the standard 0.25." — [Kalshi API changelog](https://docs.kalshi.com/changelog). A separate program returns 25% of "the total value of fees on Sports Prop Combo Markets" to makers in the component legs (confirming combos generate fees). — [Kalshi Sports Prop Combo incentive program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/17184676-sports-prop-combo-market-component-legs-liquidity-incentive-program)
- Kalshi combo vs fair-product illustration (hypothetical): legs at 50% and 35% → fair 17.5¢; a 20¢ quote implies a 2.5¢ cost. — [WTOP](https://wtop.com/prediction-markets/kalshi-combos/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by documented, fixed cost on a 2-3 leg TD parlay of fair legs (cheapest first): (1) ProphetX parlays (0% fee, but thin books and quotes from liquidity providers of unknown width); (2) Kalshi Combos (taker fee ≤1.75¢/contract, i.e., 3.5-6.5% of stake at typical 7-50¢ combo prices, plus an undocumented RFQ spread over the fair product); (3) Novig parlays (5-9% of stake embedded, plus LP margin); (4) sportsbook cross-game parlays (8.9% hold with two -110 legs, 13% with three; but anytime-TD legs are Yes-only and shaded, so realistically 15-25%); (5) Underdog 2-pick 3.5x / 3-pick 6.5x (12.5% / 18.75% on fair 1.0x legs, before line shading and correlation shifts); (6) PrizePicks 3x / 6x, Sleeper 3x / 5x, Pick6 3x / 5x (25% / 25-37.5%). The ordering within (1)-(4) is an inference, because no source measures exchange parlay quote spreads.
- For a bettor who wants exposure rather than a parlay payoff shape, the cheapest documented product is a Kalshi anytime-TD single placed as a resting (maker) order: 1¢ median spread, zero maker fee on most sports series, and prices that beat the typical sportsbook ~59% of the time (see KQ3). Splitting $20 across singles is not a parlay, so this is a product change, not a venue change.
- SGPs at DraftKings/FanDuel are the most expensive way to combine correlated TD legs (sampled underpay $6-$39 per $100 vs ~$4.50 on a -110 bet); on Kalshi same-game combos are allowed and quoted competitively by market makers, but whether quoters price correlation more generously than sportsbooks is undocumented.
- Pick'em "hold" on TD legs is higher than the fixed-table arithmetic implies because TD props on pick'em apps are rarely standard 1.0x legs: PrizePicks applies "altered" rates to Demon/Goblin and same-game lineups; Underdog applies per-pick multipliers below 1.0x and correlation shifts; Sleeper's per-leg pricing showed a ~12.9% two-way overround on one documented leg *(my arithmetic: 1/5.15 + 1/1.07 = 112.9%)*.

## Key Question 2: How do Kalshi's sports-contract fees work in 2026, and what is the effective cost of buying a "1+ touchdowns" contract at 60¢?

### Takeaway
Under the July 7, 2026 schedule, a taker pays 0.07 × P × (1−P) per contract (1.68¢ at 60¢, so an effective 61.68¢ and a 61.68% break-even), makers pay nothing on most sports series (0.0175 × P × (1−P) = 0.42¢ where a series lists a maker multiplier), and there is no settlement fee; on a $20 ticket at 60¢ the fee is about $0.54, roughly 2.8% of stake.

### Cited Findings
- Formulas and multipliers: taker M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P); maker M × 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P); default maker multiplier zero unless indicated; pro football series taker multiplier 1; "several sports series show a maker multiplier of 1"; no settlement or membership fee; schedule effective July 7, 2026. — [TrueBet, Aug 19, 2026](https://blog.truebet.app/2026/08/kalshi-fees-explained-what-a-100-sports-trade-really-costs/)
- Worked 60¢ example (20 contracts, taker): raw fee $0.3360 (33.60¢ at $0.0001 precision; 34¢ in whole-cent display); total cost $12.336; net profit if it wins $7.664; "Break-even probability: 61.68%." Rounding: schedule rounds fee plus position cost to $0.0001; API trade fees carry six decimals. — [BotForKalshi, updated Sept 16, 2026](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained)
- "Fees = round up(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P))"; 100 contracts at 10¢ cost $0.63, at 50¢ $1.75; maker fees "turned on" at 11:59 PM ET Wednesday Aug 19, 2026 per Kalshi's changelog; combo payout "maximum of $1.00 per contract" equal to the product of leg settlement values. — [Tech-Insider Kalshi payout guide (published June 5, 2026; data as of Oct 15, 2026)](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/how-does-kalshi-payout-work/)
- Per-contract fee examples: 5¢ → 0.33¢ taker / 0.08¢ maker; 50¢ → 1.75¢ / 0.44¢; 95¢ → 0.33¢ / 0.08¢. — [OddsShopper, "How does Kalshi make money," updated Oct 17, 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/how-does-kalshi-make-money)

### Inferences
- $20 ticket at 60¢ as taker *(my arithmetic)*: 32 contracts cost $19.20; fee = 0.07 × 32 × 0.6 × 0.4 = $0.5376 → ≈$0.54 (rounded up to the cent on a whole-cent display; $0.5376 at $0.0001 precision); total ≈$19.74; wins pay $32.00 (profit $12.26, i.e., 1.62x vs the nominal 1.667x at 60¢). In American-odds terms the 60¢ contract (-150) costs -161 after fee; as a resting order at 60¢ it stays -150, and resting at the 59¢ bid is -144 if filled.
- Taker fees are proportionally heavier on long shots (fee/stake = 0.07 × (1−P)): a 10¢ "2+ TD" contract costs 6.3% of stake in fees vs 2.8% at 60¢, so the maker route matters most for long-shot TD legs and multi-leg combos priced in single digits.
- Because combos carry the taker fee by default ("fee-inclusive sizing"), a 2-leg TD combo quoted at 25¢ costs ≈1.31¢/contract (5.25% of stake) in fees before any RFQ spread; makers pay nothing on combos of independent NFL games created after Aug 19, 2026.

### Gaps

- Rounding is described inconsistently (round up to the cent vs $0.0001 precision); the difference is immaterial on a $20 ticket.

### Inferences
- The direction of travel is clear: exchanges are absorbing the parlay product (Kalshi Q4 2025 parlay volume ≈$948M; Polymarket US combos since May 2026; Novig/ProphetX parlays), and pick'em apps are pivoting to peer-to-peer formats and higher payout tables under regulatory pressure, which narrows the pick'em cost disadvantage but does not eliminate it.
- Promos are the one place a $20/week bettor can reliably beat the house math for a few weeks (e.g., FanDuel 30% SGP boost tokens or Kalshi's $60-for-$25 bonus), but they are time-boxed and state-dependent; none change the structural fee ranking.
