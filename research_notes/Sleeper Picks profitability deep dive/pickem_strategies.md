# Strategies profit-focused players use on daily-fantasy pick'em apps (Sleeper Picks, PrizePicks, Underdog, Betr, DraftKings Pick6, ParlayPlay), with NFL / touchdown-prop emphasis — evidence as of Oct 2026

Scope note for the writer: everything below is sourced from 2024–Oct 2026 material except where a date is given. Numbers labelled "(my calc)" under Inferences are my arithmetic from the cited payout tables, not a source's claim. Vendor/affiliate sources (OddsShopper, OddsJam, Stokastic, Prop Professor, SmartStake, PropsEdge, upside.tools, XCLSV, SportBotAI, Turtle EV Labs, PropsBot) sell tools or earn referral fees; their ROI claims are flagged as marketing. The hard data are the state regulator filings (Massachusetts Aug 2025, Arizona 2024–2026), PrizePicks' legally required New York disclosure, the Allwyn investor deck, and the academic papers.

Important structural fact for the whole note: Sleeper is the odd one out. PrizePicks, Underdog, Betr and ParlayPlay publish a fixed payout table by number of legs (so every pick is implicitly priced at the same "even-money" price and the only decision is whether a leg's true probability clears the table's per-leg breakeven). Sleeper instead attaches its own multiplier to each side of each prop (e.g., an anytime-TD "over" at 4.50x vs a reception "over" at 1.65x), and the entry multiplier is the product of the leg multipliers, with a house margin folded into every leg — [OddsShopper, Jul 30 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/comparisons/how-to-play-sleeper-picks); [OddsShopper NFL guide, Jul 14 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/sleeper-fantasy-football-promo-code). That difference drives almost every answer below.

## Q1. "+EV" line shopping: how sharps devig to find mispriced pick'em lines, edge thresholds, how many legs, documented ROI/hit rates

### Takeaway
The documented method is: take a sharp two-way market (Pinnacle or a sportsbook consensus) at the same line the app posts, strip the vig (multiplicative or power/"worst-case"), and play a leg only when its no-vig probability beats the slip's per-leg breakeven (≈54–59% on PrizePicks/Underdog, computed per-leg on Sleeper from each multiplier). Experienced players favor 4–6-leg Flex entries because breakevens are lowest there (≈54.2% on PrizePicks 5/6-pick Flex vs 57.7% on a 2-pick Power) and want ≥55–58% true probability per leg; no independent, audited long-run ROI record exists, and the only hard disclosures (PrizePicks' New York data: ~18–23% of contestants are net winners over a week/month) show most players lose.

### Cited Findings

Breakeven tables (fixed-payout apps)

Formula for all-or-nothing slips: breakeven = (1/M)^(1/n); Flex solved numerically. 4-pick Power is harder to clear than 3- or 5-pick Power — [SmartStake](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/how-to-beat-prizepicks)

Breakevens: 57.7%, 58.5%, 56.2%, 59.1%, 56.9%, 54.3%, 54.2% vs 52.4% for a −110 sportsbook line. Payouts vary by state; check the app before submitting — [PropsEdge](https://www.propsedge.io/blog/how-to-win-on-prizepicks). (Note the Power-3 breakeven conflicts: 55.0% at SmartStake vs 58.5% at PropsEdge, because PropsEdge assumes 5x and SmartStake assumes a higher current multiplier.)

- Underdog breakevens (GamedayMath, Jul 2023, older tables): 2-pick Standard 57.7%, 3-pick 55.03%, 4-pick 56.23%, 5-pick 54.93%; Insured 3/4/5-pick 57.74%/55.03%/54.75% — [GamedayMath](https://www.gamedaymath.com/blog/underdog-fantasy-payout-math). - Upside.tools (Aug 18 2026) converts whole tables to a single per-leg price: PrizePicks ≈ −119 per leg (54.34% breakeven); Underdog 4-man Flex (7.2x top prize plus a 3-of-4 consolation) ≈ −107 per leg (51.69%); a 2.65-point per-leg gap in Underdog's favor. A true 4-leg parlay at −107 would pay ~14x vs Underdog's 7.2x — [upside.tools](https://upside.tools/research/prizepicks-vs-underdog-pricing)

- Betr Picks fixed multipliers run from 3x on 2 picks to 100x on 8 picks (up to 10,000x on the largest entries); ParlayPlay tops out at 155x on a 9-pick All-In and sells insurance on misses — [Rotowire](https://www.rotowire.com/news/betr-picks-vs-parlayplay-which-pickem-app-should-you-use-140423). DraftKings Pick6 is a peer pool, not a fixed table: 70% of the pool to 6/6, 20% to 5/6, 10% to 4/6 — [PromoGuy](https://www.promoguy.us/reviews/draftkings-fantasy/pick6/pick6-payout/); DraftKings shows an estimated payout, ~35:1 for 6/6 in a 2026 review — [FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/how-to-dominate-draftkings-pick6/)

Sleeper: breakeven is per leg, from the multiplier
- Each side of every prop has its own multiplier scaled to likelihood with the margin built in; examples from a June 2026 card: Bryce Harper over 0.5 HR 3.22x (≈ +222), Goldschmidt over 0.5 SB 5.15x vs under 1.07x, Greinke over 2.5 K 2.02x; entries $1–$500, up to 8 legs, payouts "as high as 100x," max win $5,000 per entry (per this review). Author's verdict: dynamic pricing "usually prices props worse than a traditional sportsbook" and "if you want the most +EV play on every single leg, Sleeper probably is not your app" — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/comparisons/how-to-play-sleeper-picks)
- NFL examples (illustrative, Jul 14 2026): rotational RB anytime TD ≈ 4.50x (≈ one-in-five, ≈ +350 at a book); dual-threat QB over 39.5 rush yds ≈ 1.75x; WR1 over 5.5 receptions ≈ 1.65x; longest reception 30+ ≈ 3.00x. "If a sportsbook offers +400 on the same anytime TD that Sleeper prices at 4.50x, the sportsbook number is better." Advice: remove the margin to find a fair price, compare to the best market price, lead with volume stats, limit long shots to one per card — [OddsShopper NFL](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/sleeper-fantasy-football-promo-code)
- Sleeper's current rules (updated Feb 13 2026): 2–8 picks per entry; entries go into pools of up to 5,000; first place in a regular pool pays 100x (3x in beginner/intermediate pools, $1 max entry there); regular pools allow entries up to $500; "a single user may not account for more than 5% of any individual pool's total entries"; guaranteed payout = entry fee × VS Score once the required correct-pick count is met; the higher of pool payout or guaranteed payout applies; "all contests must include players from multiple teams"; Sleeper "reserves the right to void, change, or remove any pick at its sole discretion" — [Sleeper rules](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9047931-sleeper-player-picks-rules); [RotoGrinders PicksVS guide, Mar 2026](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/sleeper-promo-code/picksvs)
- Flex on Sleeper requires 3+ picks, a minimum 1.25x perfect-entry multiplier, pays with one miss, and with two misses only on 5+ picks; voided picks are regraded as if never included — [Sleeper Flex rules](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9261402-sleeper-player-picks-flex-contests)
- A real 7-pick Sleeper Max entry (Dec 4 2025) where all seven hit paid only 2.70x — 1.80x base multiplier plus a 50% boost token — because every leg was heavily favored; max is 1000x on an 8-pick Max per this source (conflicts with the $5,000/100x figures above) — [FantasyLabs, Oct 12 2026](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/sleeper-fantasy/)

Devig method
- SmartStake's calculator uses multiplicative devig: implied prob = 1/decimal odds, divide each by the overround; example LeBron over 25.5 at −140 / under +118 on Pinnacle → 56.0% true for the over (4.21% margin) — [SmartStake](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/how-to-beat-prizepicks)
- Outlier describes two routes: convert each leg's fair price to a percentage and average vs the slip threshold, or compute the geometric-mean per-leg rate the payout requires — [Outlier](https://help.outlier.bet/en/articles/13003913-prizepicks-strategy-how-to-turn-fixed-parlays-into-a-ev-game)
- Devig methods: multiplicative, additive, power, Shin; "worst-case" takes the longest fair value among them; the spread between methods widens as juice rises (props carry more juice than mainlines) — [CrazyNinjaMike devigger help](http://crazyninjamike.com/public/sportsbooks/sportsbook_devigger_help.aspx); [BetHero](https://betherosports.com/blog/devigging-methods-explained); Pinnacle margin on less-liquid markets ~3–5% — [Pinnacle Odds Dropper](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/guides/how-to-devig-pinnacle-s-odds-for-betting-on-soft-books)
- Caveat on the method's premise: a flagged "+4% EV" is "mathematically correct only if sportsbook consensus is the true line" — [XCLSV OddsJam review via search](https://xclsvmedia.com/oddsjam-fantasy-optimizer-review-2026-prizepicks-underdog-dfs-tool/)
- PrizePicks publishes "a payout type, not a price": of 473 PrizePicks NFL rows scraped Oct 16 2026, 362 were over-only (240 demon, 117 goblin, 5 standard); only 96 of 125 player/stat pairs had a standard two-sided line; one QB (C.J. Stroud) had nine passing-yard lines, eight over-only. Underdog had exactly one row per player/stat (127/127). The two apps agreed exactly on only 17 of 46 comparable lines — [dev.to analysis](https://dev.to/nikita_iakovlev_415524c19/prizepicks-gave-me-nine-pass-yard-lines-for-one-quarterback-eight-are-over-only-3fed)

- PropsEdge: play Power slips only on picks above ~58%; 5- and 6-pick Flex only on picks above ~55%; "six legs at 54% lose while six at 58% can profit"; on three picks use Flex 3 or drop the third leg; don't add a weak leg to chase correlation. - Unabated: "you want to be playing four-, five- and six-man entries"; Dragiev plays a two-man only when no reasonable five-man exists; hypothetical at 60% per leg: Underdog 5-leg Standard (20x) edge 62.3%, PrizePicks 6-leg Flex (25x) "far greater"; Underdog Insured has a lower breakeven but Standard has the better edge; the tool example was a WNBA under at ~33% edge and ~73% hit rate — [Unabated](https://unabated.com/post/art-and-science-of-dfs-pickem-strategy)
- SmartStake: Flex slips are "often more efficient for a long-run edge" (lower breakeven, lower variance); Power suits larger-edge legs; illustrative 6-pick Flex at 56% per leg returns $117.60 per $100 (+17.6%); 4-pick Power at 58/57/59/56% returns +9.2% with a 10.9% chance of hitting — [SmartStake](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/how-to-beat-prizepicks)
- A limited (sharp) PrizePicks player's regimen: only 3-pick Power, 5-pick Flex and 6-pick Flex; no 2-pick, 4-pick, demons or goblins; only legs priced in his favor vs de-vigged sportsbook prices; leans to overs at identical prices because of the reboot/void rule — [OddsShopper "PrizePicks Limits," Aug 24 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/prizepicks-limits)

### Inferences
- Hold at coin-flip picks implied by the PrizePicks table (my calc, p = 50% per leg, independent): Power 2 (3x) → 25% hold; Power 3 (5x) → 37.5%; Power 4 (10x) → 37.5%; Flex 3 (2.25/1.25) → 25%; Flex 4 (5/1.5) → 31%; Flex 5 (10/2/0.4) → 25%; Flex 6 (25/2/0.4) → 33%. Underdog Pick-5 at 20x → 37.5%. These are why the breakeven is ~54–58% rather than 52.4%: the app is charging roughly a 25–37% parlay-style margin on uninformed play, which matches the 15–30% "implied hold" range modelled by [track360 (Jun 2026)](https://track360.io/blog/pick-em-dfs-prizepicks-underdog-operator-affiliate-model-2026) and the ~21–25% actual holds in regulator data (Q6).
- Why 5/6-pick Flex is the sharp default: the consolation tiers make the payoff function convex in the per-leg hit rate, so the breakeven is lowest (≈54.2%) and the ROI at a given edge is highest (PropsEdge: +43%/+66% at 60% vs +8% on a 2-pick). The cost is variance: even at 60% per leg a 6-pick hits perfect only 0.6^6 ≈ 4.7% of the time (my calc), so bankroll swings are large for a $20 player.
- Unabated's "62.3% edge" for a 5-leg Underdog at 60% does not reproduce as simple ROI: 0.6^5 × 20 = 1.555, i.e. +55.5% (my calc). Their figure likely includes their push adjustment or a different edge definition; the direction (Flex-6 on PrizePicks > Standard-5 on Underdog at the same edge) is consistent across sources.
- On Sleeper there is no table breakeven; each leg's breakeven is 1/multiplier. The 4.50x ATTD example implies 22.2% with margin; a +400 sportsbook price implies 20.0%; if the sportsbook ATTD market itself holds ~19–23% (Q5), the fair probability is closer to 16–17%, making the 4.50x leg roughly −25% EV (my calc). That is the arithmetic behind the repeated advice that Sleeper long shots are "almost always worse than a traditional sportsbook."
- Sleeper's 7-pick 1.80x example implies an average leg multiplier of 1.80^(1/7) ≈ 1.088x, i.e. ~92% implied probability per leg including margin; at a true 90% per leg the 7-leg hits 47.8% of the time → EV ≈ −14%; at 88% → −26% (my calc). Stacking heavy favorites on Sleeper multiplies seven small margins.
- For the $20, 2–8-pick recreational profile: the documented consensus is 3-pick Power or 5/6-pick Flex on PrizePicks/Underdog with every leg ≥55–58% no-vig; on Sleeper, only legs whose multiplier exceeds the best sportsbook's no-vig price, which the two Sleeper-focused guides say is rare.

### Gaps
- No independent, audited multi-season ROI record for any pick'em +EV strategy was found; everything is vendor-reported, single-user, or a one-month anecdote. Reddit is blocked to this agent's search tool, so tracked spreadsheets posted there could not be retrieved.
- No source gives Sleeper-specific breakeven/hold figures or a devig workflow tuned to Sleeper's per-leg multipliers; the Sleeper guides assert prices are worse than sportsbooks without a measured sample.
- The current PrizePicks payout tables differ by state and changed with the Aug 2025 "Arena" format; sources disagree on the Power-3 multiplier (5x vs higher), so breakevens should be rechecked in-app.

## Q2. Correlation: using correlated legs (same game, QB+WR, totals + props) against fixed payouts, and whether it still works on Sleeper's per-pick multipliers

### Takeaway
Correlation is the classic way to beat a fixed-payout table (the table assumes independence, so positively correlated legs raise the probability of a perfect slip without changing the payout), but as of 2025–2026 the big two apps explicitly reprice it: Underdog shows a lowered multiplier for positively correlated picks at slip-building time, and PrizePicks says lineups with multiple athletes in the same game "may have reduced payout rates." Sleeper publishes no correlation rule; its multi-team requirement, 1.0x "ez Pick" filler, and per-side multipliers mean the correlation edge there is whatever the pricer fails to capture, and no source has measured it.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: PrizePicks pays a fixed multiple whether legs are independent or move together, so legs that win together (e.g., QB passing yards + his top receiver's receiving yards) can be an edge; avoid negatively correlated pairs (WR yards more + his QB's interceptions more) unless each leg stands alone; the app restricts some same-game combinations and the rules change — [PropsEdge](https://www.propsedge.io/blog/how-to-win-on-prizepicks)
- PrizePicks official payout page: "Specific Pick Combinations: Lineups with multiple athletes playing in the same game may have reduced payout rates"; "Lineups including Demon or Goblin projections or other discounted projections carry altered standard payout rates" — [PrizePicks Help Center: Payouts](https://www.prizepicks.com/help-center/payouts); a related help article says same-team/same-game "same-game combinations" produce "unique payout rates," often lower, visible in "My Lineups" before submission — [PrizePicks "Payouts Explained" via search snippet](https://intercom.help/prizepicks/en/articles/9047668-payouts-explained)
- Underdog official (via search snippet; page blocks fetching): correlated projections cause "payout shifting," and "this increase or decrease in your payout multiplier will be displayed when building your entry"; positively correlated picks (QB passing yards + WR receiving yards) decrease the multiplier; negatively correlated picks (QB passing yards + rival receiver's yards) can move it the other way; applies to Streaks and Flex too — [Underdog help](https://help.underdogsports.com/en/articles/11010091-correlated-picks-how-stacking-players-affects-your-payout-multiplier)
- Stokastic (Aug 17 2026): "PrizePicks and Underdog will shade the payout down" on correlated same-team picks; take correlated slips only if still +EV after the reduction; pace (~99–100 possessions/48 min in NBA) and vacated usage are the drivers; two high-usage scorers on one team can cannibalize each other; a ~10-point projected blowout creates shared downside — [Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/nba-pickem-correlation-strategy)
- Unabated's worked example of correlation changing PrizePicks payouts: a 6-man golf entry with three players' total strokes "fewer" plus the same players' birdies "more" pays 15x on 6/6; swapping to birdies "fewer" pays 30x. Dragiev avoids simple correlations (teammate under-assists + over-points) "unless the edges are crazy" because the market is still inefficient enough on single legs; Unabated's Entry Builder does not model correlation — [Unabated](https://unabated.com/post/art-and-science-of-dfs-pickem-strategy)
- Sleeper's only related rules: "all contests must include players from multiple teams"; a player may not appear more than once in an entry; no correlation pricing rule is stated — [Sleeper rules](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9047931-sleeper-player-picks-rules). If you pick only one team, Sleeper auto-adds an "ez Pick" from the opposing team at 1.0x that "always grades as a win" (injury-insured) and is removed once you have 2+ players from 2+ teams; entry limits "vary by user and sport"; you need a minimum 1.25x entry multiplier with an ez Pick — [Sleeper ez Pick, Jul 15 2026](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/15382178-what-is-an-ez-pick). Sleeper also sells "Combos" (a Team Pick plus 2+ Player Picks rolled into one combined payout) — [Sleeper Combos](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/15396866-what-are-combos)
- Sleeper comparison article: the three apps' correlation handling is not explained; Sleeper's payout "does not always sit on one simple fixed table" and depends on contest type, boosts and scoring — [Stokastic comparison, Jul 21 2026](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/prizepicks-vs-underdog-vs-sleeper)
- Size of NFL correlations (season-long fantasy points, not single-game props): QB-to-top-WR ≈ +0.31; same-team WR pairs ≈ 0; higher figures come from single-game slates — [Rotowire, 2026](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/does-stacking-work-in-fantasy-football-what-four-years-of-data-say-about-drafting-correlated-players-2026-131409)
- Academic: in college football 2005–2015, favorites covering the spread were more likely to push games over the total, an exploitable correlation books were "too conservative" about — [Davis, Dawson & Krieger, J. Prediction Markets 2018](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Correlated-parlay-betting-An-analysis-of/99380090298406600)
- Prediction-market parlays: on Kalshi (12,639 cross-game parlays, Apr 29–May 13 2026) 2–4-leg parlays priced at ~0.99–1.00 of the product of leg prices, but 5 legs ≈ 1.01, 8 legs ≈ 1.07, 10 legs ≈ 1.22, 11 legs ≈ 1.31 (~3% per leg markup, R² ≈ 0.94) — [Moshrefi, arXiv Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14430v1)
- Passing-TD markets as a correlation input: the over on 1.5 passing TDs hit 45.9% of 1,021 starts (2024–25); 0 TDs in ~21% of starts — [OddsShopper passing TD, Sep 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/nfl-passing-touchdown-props)

### Inferences
- On fixed-payout apps the "free" correlation edge has largely been closed by explicit repricing; what remains is (a) correlations the pricer doesn't model (cross-game, weather, pace, blowout/benching, injury-redistribution) and (b) negative-correlation penalties you can avoid. That matches Dragiev's stance: single-leg mispricing is still the bigger edge, so only take correlation when it's free or nearly so.
- On Sleeper, because each leg carries its own margin and the entry is a product, correlated legs don't change the payout the way a table does; the question is whether Sleeper shades the multiplier of the second same-game leg. No source documents that. The ez Pick rule means a same-team QB+WR stack can satisfy the multi-team requirement without adding a real leg (the filler is 1.0x and auto-wins), so Sleeper structurally permits same-team stacking — but the ez Pick article also implies Sleeper limits are per-user, which is the usual counter.
- For TD props specifically, a QB passing-TD over is positively correlated with his receivers' anytime-TD "more" and negatively with his own rushing-TD; on PrizePicks the ATTD "more" is usually a Demon (Q5), so a QB+WR TD stack combines a repriced same-game penalty with a demon's altered payout — a double haircut.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much PrizePicks or Underdog reduce the multiplier for a given correlated pair (e.g., QB yards + WR yards) in NFL; both say "check the slip."
- No source states whether Sleeper adjusts multipliers for correlated same-game picks, and no measured record of correlated-slip performance on Sleeper exists.
- No single-game (not season-long) correlation coefficients for NFL player-prop pairs were found.

## Q3. Promotions/boosts: how much of documented profitability comes from deposit matches, boosts, free squares and promo abuse

### Takeaway
Every documented "winner" in the secondhand Reddit sample was a bonus user, the one quantified promo EV estimate (~$19 expected on a $5 PrizePicks entry) is larger than a $20 player's realistic per-entry edge, and the academic literature ties promotions to parlay adoption and larger losses — so for a small-stakes player, first-deposit matches and boost tokens are the most reliable positive EV available, but they are one-time, carry playthrough and expiry, and the app's margin is still embedded in every pick you must play through.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Value of a matched bonus (my framework): with a 1x playthrough on bonus funds only, expected cash ≈ bonus × (1 − house hold per entry) if you play at the app's average margin, so a $100 match is worth roughly $75–$85 if the realized hold is 15–25% (Q6), and ≈ bonus × (1 + your EV) if you play genuinely +EV legs. Sleeper's $120 package is therefore worth on the order of $90–$110 in expectation; the $20 "free" portion is pure EV. If the playthrough covers deposit + bonus (one reading of Underdog's terms), the value drops by the hold on the deposit as well.
- Scale check for the target reader (my calc): three $20 entries a week for an 18-week NFL season is $1,080 of handle; at an optimistic +5% ROI that is ~$54 of expected profit for the season — less than the EV of a single first-deposit promo on any one app. That is the quantitative sense in which "promotions, not picking, are where small-stakes profit comes from," and it is also why the only sharp regimen documented (OddsShopper's limited player) leans on correlated structure and price, not promos, at higher volume.
- Boost tokens (Sleeper 50% boost, Underdog/PrizePicks profit boosts) convert a −EV slip into +EV only when the boost exceeds the embedded margin; a 50% boost on a 1.80x slip (→ 2.70x) more than covers a 25% hold, so the documented 7-pick example was likely +EV even though its unboosted form (1.80x on seven favorites) was not.

## Q5. Softest vs hardest prop markets, and why TD markets are called high-variance/high-vig

### Takeaway
Anytime-TD is the highest-margin common NFL prop (one Oct 2026 measurement: ~19–23% hold on 768 priced players vs ~4.5% on sides/totals, with a 9–29% confidence range), it is one-sided (no "under"), so it can't be devigged the normal way and must be priced against a model or consensus; its binary, Poisson-like outcome produces extreme variance. On pick'em apps the ATTD "more" is sold as a PrizePicks Demon or a Sleeper long-shot multiplier (≈4.5x), both described as worse than the best sportsbook price. Passing-TD lines barely move (only 1.5 or 2.5 exist), so disagreement shows up in price, which creates shopping gaps that pick'em apps sometimes sit at the bottom of.

### Cited Findings
- Measured ATTD hold: on 768 priced players checked against ESPN box scores for 2026 Weeks 1–4, prices paid back "only about 81 cents" of implied chance — a hold of ~19–23% depending on the paragraph (18.5% per leg in one place; 95% range 9%–29%); a standard side/total holds ~4.5%; published estimates for ATTD are 6–10%; ATTD prices "differ more between books than most NFL markets"; real TD parlay tickets over the same weeks did worse than the math — [DFS Dashboard, Oct 16 2026](https://dfsdashboard.com/dfs-classic-lineup-builder-use-to-bet-like-a-parlay/) (DFS-marketing site; feed prices "may not match any one sportsbook")
- One-sidedness: "there is no under" for ATTD props, so standard two-sided devig fails and the comparison must be against other books; the cheapest-to-dearest book gap on a comparable longshot market "ran about seven points of implied probability"; defensive TDs allowed to WRs ranged 0.41–1.53/game (3.7x spread) and to TEs 0.18–1.00 (5.5x) across 32 teams in 2025; the model floor is 20 games while the median player had 12 games in September — [Steezanomics, Sep–Oct 2026](https://steezanomics.com/learn/what-to-check-for-anytime-touchdown-props/)
- Outlier: TD props are "high variance"; avoid overfitting 2–3-game streaks; shop (+165 vs +185 on the same player); inputs are red-zone targets/rush attempts, goal-line share, opponent TD-allowed rates, injuries; low-total games can hide underpriced longshots — [Outlier TD props, Jul 2026](https://help.outlier.bet/en/articles/12428522-best-strategy-to-bet-nfl-touchdown-props)
- Passing TDs: 1,021 QB starts (20+ passes) in 2024–25 average 1.50 passing TDs; distribution 0: 21.1%, 1: 33.0%, 2: 28.2%, 3: 11.7%, 4: 4.8%, 5+: 1.3% vs Poisson 22.3/33.5/25.1/12.5/4.7/1.9; over 1.5 hits 45.9%; fair over 1.5 ≈ +118 and over 2.5 ≈ +464; "the line never moves" because only 1.5 and 2.5 exist, so Goff's 1.5 was priced from −121 to −150 across 28 sources; at best prices Prescott's two-way hold was 0.2%; Underdog's over on Rodgers (+145) sat at the bottom of a $51-per-$100 gap vs the top price — [OddsShopper, Sep 23 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/nfl-passing-touchdown-props)
- PrizePicks ATTD: line is 0.5 Anytime TDs; for most players the "More" is a Demon (higher multiplier, harder tier), for top RBs (Gibbs, Robinson) the More can be a Goblin; the "Less" is generally a Goblin; rushing, receiving, return and fumble-recovery TDs count, passing TDs do not — [PrizePicks playbook, Sep 2026](https://www.prizepicks.com/playbook-article/what-does-anytime-touchdown-scorer-mean-how-to-pick-nfl-td-scorers); in the scraped board, allowedPicks was null on every "Anytime TDs (Combo)" row — [dev.to](https://dev.to/nikita_iakovlev_415524c19/prizepicks-gave-me-nine-pass-yard-lines-for-one-quarterback-eight-are-over-only-3fed)
- Sleeper ATTD ≈ 4.50x for a rotational RB vs ≈ +350 to +400 at a book; "most Sleeper prices are worse than the open market"; keep long shots (ATTD, 30+ yard receptions) to one per card; lead with volume stats (QB rush yards, WR receptions, RB rush yards in favorable scripts); wind >15 mph favors run over passing overs — [OddsShopper NFL](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/sleeper-fantasy-football-promo-code)
- Prediction markets are often the best ATTD price: weekly Covers ATTD articles benchmark against Kalshi (e.g., Denzel Boston 3.1x vs a 2.85x buy-to price in Week 6 2026) — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/nfl/anytime-touchdown-picks-week-6-2026); OddsShopper argues Kalshi "routinely beats your sportsbook" on TD prices (promotional, small sample) — [OddsShopper Kalshi](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)
- General prop vig: books take heavier juice on props, "sometimes starting at −115/−115," and often raise juice on a popular side instead of moving the number — [CBS Sports via search](https://cbssports.com/betting/news/college-football); the public overpays for recognizable names, so goal-line backs are often the sharper ATTD price and ATTD is "the market where line shopping pays the most" — [OddsShopper NFL props explained](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/nfl-player-props)
- Which markets pick'em pricers like: PrizePicks "props pay close to even money, so the board leans toward stats where both sides are relatively close in likelihood" (receptions, yards), while Sleeper's per-side multipliers are what allow it to list long-shot stats (HRs, SBs, TDs) at all — [OddsShopper Sleeper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/comparisons/how-to-play-sleeper-picks)

### Inferences
- "High-vig" and "high-variance" are two separate problems: the hold (~19–23% measured) means even a perfect model needs a large edge to overcome it, and the binary Poisson outcome (a 20% scorer misses 80% of the time) means a $20 player's results are dominated by luck over any season-length sample (my reading of the DFS Dashboard and Steezanomics numbers).
- Softest documented NFL prop markets for pick'em are the volume stats (receptions, rushing/receiving yards, QB rush yards) where the apps post two-sided lines near even money and sportsbooks post liquid two-way markets to devig against; hardest are ATTD and alt-TD markets where the app's own tier (Demon) or multiplier (Sleeper) already embeds a long-shot margin and there is no clean two-sided reference. Passing-TD overs/unders sit in between: two-sided, Poisson-modelable, but with a 2–3 point implied-probability spread between the 1.5 and 2.5 lines, which is why shopping matters and why a pick'em app's fixed −119-equivalent price on a 45.9%-hit over 1.5 is a bad deal unless the app posts the 1.5 when the market has moved to 2.5.

### Gaps
- No source measures hold or realized hit rate for ATTD or passing-TD picks specifically on Sleeper, PrizePicks or Underdog; the 19–23% figure is sportsbook-feed based.
- No source documents PrizePicks' actual Demon multiplier for an ATTD "more" or how it compares to a sportsbook price on the same player.
- Whether Underdog currently lists 0.5 ATTD lines, and at what effective price, was not confirmed.

- 547M NFL wagers (2018–2023): heavier parlay use is associated with larger losses after controlling for volume and stake — [Borghesi et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326007464)

Limiting of winners
- PrizePicks: a long-time user (joined May 2021; 1,902 winning entries, 21,100 copies by others, $71,977 lifetime gross won — not net) is "capped at $5 a card" with $25 maximum exposure on any single player across open entries — [OddsShopper, Aug 24 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/prizepicks-limits); another user's max entry dropped to $5 per slip with no email — [upside.tools, Aug 1 2026](https://upside.tools/research/prizepicks-limited-max-entry) (both anecdotal; the second asserts, without data, that apps "do not limit people who lose" and that duplicate accounts get detected)
- Underdog official: "Underdog Fantasy may impose additional limits on accounts," including "a limit to the dollar amount that a customer can play on a single player's projections" and "a limit to the dollar amount that a user can play on a single entry," "variable depending on the sport and … subject to change" — [Underdog "Pick'em Restrictions – Limited Player" via search snippet](https://help.underdogsports.com/en/articles/10993883-pick-em-restrictions-limited-player)
- Sleeper official: "Entry limits … vary by user and sport"; a single user may not exceed 5% of a pool's entries; regular pools max $500 per entry; beginner/intermediate pools ($1 max) exclude "highly experienced users" — [Sleeper ez Pick](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/15382178-what-is-an-ez-pick); [Sleeper rules](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9047931-sleeper-player-picks-rules)
- Secondhand: Redditors reported caps after winning ~$1,200 over a year or two straight $100 months, with max entry dropping to $1k or less — [SportBotAI](https://www.sportbotai.com/blog/is-prizepicks-worth-it-reddit) (unlinked)

### Inferences
- The regulator holds (≈20–25% of entry fees for PrizePicks/Underdog in two states, two years apart, before and after the switch to "peer-to-peer") are consistent with the fixed-table math in Q1 (25–37% at coin-flip play) minus the effect of promos and some player skill; they are 2–5x the hold on straight sportsbook bets and in line with sportsbook parlay/SGP holds. The "peer-to-peer" relabeling did not reduce the take.
- Reconciling NY's 18–23% monthly net winners with a ~21% hold: the winner share is dominated by short-horizon variance (a quarter of players are up in any given week), while the hold is the long-run drain; the concentration data (top 1% of winners take ~10–12% of all entry fees) say that a thin layer of players extracts meaningful money — which is exactly the population the apps then cap at $5.
- Sleeper's hold cannot be read from any filing (its MA volume was ~$6K; it is not broken out in AZ coverage), but its per-leg multipliers are described as worse than sportsbook prices by every guide that examined them, so there is no evidence its effective hold is lower than PrizePicks'/Underdog's.
- Arithmetic for the reader (my calc): at a 21% hold, a $20 entry costs ~$4.20 in expectation; 3 entries/week for an NFL season ≈ −$227 expected without an edge.

### Gaps
- No regulator breaks out Sleeper's entry fees and prizes (AZ coverage names only PrizePicks/DraftKings/Underdog; MA shows Sleeper at $6,255 gross), so Sleeper's realized hold is unknown.
- No threshold (win rate, CLV, profit) that triggers limits on any app is documented; the "~55% win rate" trigger seen in one comparison is unsourced. No case of Sleeper limiting a specific winner was found.
- Michigan's per-operator 2025 figures could not be mapped to operators from the PDF extraction and MI reports no entry fees.
- The Borghesi papers are paywalled; figures come from abstracts/summaries.

## Q7. Behavioral pitfalls: chasing long parlays, stacking favorites, misreading multiplier products

### Takeaway
The documented ways recreational pick'em players lose are structural rather than analytical: longer slips carry a bigger markup (Kalshi parlays rise from fair at 2–4 legs to +22% overpriced at 10 legs; PrizePicks' 6-pick Flex holds ~33% at coin-flip), stacking heavy favorites on a multiplier product like Sleeper compounds seven small margins into a 1.8x payout, one-sided Demon/Goblin and "over-only" rows are mistaken for two-sided prices, and bettors systematically overestimate their own returns (predicting break-even, losing 7.5%).

### Cited Findings
- Parlay markup grows with legs: Kalshi parlays priced ≈ fair at 2–4 legs, 1.07x at 8, 1.22x at 10, 1.31x at 11 legs (~3%/leg) — [Moshrefi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14430v1); sportsbook parlay hold anecdotally 20–30% vs much lower on straights; "parlays are especially confusing to consumers" — [Brown et al.](https://mattbrownecon.github.io/assets/papers/jmp/sportsbetting.pdf); bettors migrated to multi-leg contracts with higher margins and lost more — [Borghesi et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326007464)
- Overoptimism: average high-volume bettor predicts break-even, loses 7.5 cents per dollar; self-control problems present but "quantitatively smaller" — [Brown et al.](https://mattbrownecon.github.io/assets/papers/jmp/sportsbetting.pdf)
- Stacking favorites on Sleeper: a 7-pick where all legs hit paid 1.80x base (2.70x with a 50% boost) because "the payout reflected how heavily favored each leg was, not the leg count" — [FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/sleeper-fantasy/); Sleeper requires a 1.25x minimum multiplier for Flex, so favorite-heavy Flex entries can be ineligible — [Sleeper Flex](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9261402-sleeper-player-picks-flex-contests)
- Misreading one-sided products: 362 of 473 PrizePicks NFL rows were over-only Demons/Goblins; "treating these as symmetric over/under lines would model bets the app does not sell"; PrizePicks publishes "a payout type, not a price" — [dev.to](https://dev.to/nikita_iakovlev_415524c19/prizepicks-gave-me-nine-pass-yard-lines-for-one-quarterback-eight-are-over-only-3fed); a Demon "doesn't guarantee 2,000x" — [PrizePicks](https://www.prizepicks.com/demons-and-goblins); Demon/Goblin lineups "carry altered standard payout rates" and same-game lineups "may have reduced payout rates" — [PrizePicks payouts](https://www.prizepicks.com/help-center/payouts)
- Slip-size traps: 4-pick Power needs 56.2% per leg, harder than 3- or 5-pick Power (55.0%/54.9%) — [SmartStake](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/how-to-beat-prizepicks); a Power 3 needs 58.5% per pick vs 54.2% on a 6-pick Flex; six legs at 54% lose — [PropsEdge](https://www.propsedge.io/blog/how-to-win-on-prizepicks); a 55.8% hit rate loses 8.6% on 2-picks but earns 9.26% on 6-pick Flex — [Rotowire](https://www.rotowire.com/betting/oddsjam-review)
- Grading rules that quietly cut payouts: on PrizePicks a DNP removes the projection and lowers the payout by one tier, and a tie "revert[s] your payout down by one level" — [PrizePicks DNP/ties via search](https://www.prizepicks.com/help-center/dnps-reboots-and-ties); on Sleeper a push is a final stat equal to the projection, voided legs in 3+ pick entries are "graded with adjusted payouts," and Sleeper may remove any pick at its discretion — [Sleeper rules](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9047931-sleeper-player-picks-rules); Underdog users report "occasional removal of legs mid-slate" — [XCLSV via search](https://xclsvmedia.com/prizepicks-vs-underdog-vs-draftkings-pick6-2026/)
- Variance under-appreciation: a 6-pick Flex at 54% hits perfect ~2.5% of the time — [PropsEdge](https://www.propsedge.io/blog/how-to-win-on-prizepicks); a 4-pick Power with a real 9.2% edge still hits only 10.9% of the time — [SmartStake](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/how-to-beat-prizepicks); TD props are "high variance" and 2–3-game streaks overfit — [Outlier](https://help.outlier.bet/en/articles/12428522-best-strategy-to-bet-nfl-touchdown-props)
- Favorite-longshot behavior: the public overpays for recognizable names on ATTD — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/nfl-player-props); Kalshi contracts near expiry show step-like calibration consistent with insurance-demand behavior — [Moshrefi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14430v1)
- Net result for the population: 70–76% of PrizePicks NY contestants are net losers over a week/month — [PrizePicks NY](https://www.prizepicks.com/new-york-prize-data)

### Inferences
- The "8-pick for 100x/1000x" marketing on Sleeper/Betr/ParlayPlay is the pick'em version of the long-parlay markup: at Sleeper the product of eight margined multipliers is where the 1000x headline comes from, and at Betr/ParlayPlay the fixed 100x–155x tables imply per-leg breakevens around 56–57% (my calc: (1/100)^(1/8) = 56.2%; (1/155)^(1/9) = 57.1%) on legs the player cannot price.
- "Stacking favorites" is a distinct trap on each product: on fixed tables it is harmless to the payout but every favorite is still priced at the same −119-equivalent (so a 70% leg is given away at 54% odds — bad for the player only in that it wastes a slot); on Sleeper the favorite's multiplier is tiny and carries full margin, so a card of favorites is a near-certain small loss.
- For the $20, 2–8-pick reader the documented behavioral rules reduce to: never pay for leg count (stay at 3-pick Power or 5/6-pick Flex), never take a Demon/Goblin or a Sleeper long shot without a sportsbook/exchange price to compare, treat one-sided rows as priced offers rather than lines, and expect long losing streaks even when the math is right.

### Gaps
- No pick'em-specific behavioral study (equivalent to Brown et al. or Borghesi et al.) was found; the parlay literature is sportsbook/exchange based and applied here by analogy.
- No data on how often recreational pick'em users choose 6–8-leg entries versus 2–3-leg entries, or the hit-rate distribution of real user slips, beyond PrizePicks' NY net-winner shares.
