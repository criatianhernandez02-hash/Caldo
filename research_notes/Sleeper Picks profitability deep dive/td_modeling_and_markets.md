# NFL Touchdown-Prop Modeling and Market Accuracy (anytime TD, passing-TD totals), as of 2026-10-17

Scope note for the writer: this note covers (1) which features predict player touchdowns and which modeling families are used, (2) how "expected touchdowns" (xTD) is computed in ffopportunity/nflverse and whether xTD beats raw TDs as a predictor, (3) published model accuracy (Brier/log loss/hit rates) versus sportsbook-implied probabilities, (4) structural efficiency of the anytime-TD market, (5) Kalshi/Polymarket prop pricing versus sportsbooks, and (6) free/cheap historical prop-odds data for backtesting. Dates matter: Kalshi listed its first NFL touchdown-scorer contracts on 2025-09-04, Polymarket began charging sports taker fees in Feb 2026, and Kalshi's current fee schedule is dated 2026-07-07. Almost all "model vs. market" evidence is self-published (GitHub, blogs, vendor studies); I found no peer-reviewed study of NFL player-prop (TD) market efficiency. Quality flags are given inline.

## Key Question 1: What features matter most for predicting whether a player scores a TD this week, and what is the evidence?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence points to (a) field-position-weighted opportunity (goal-line carries, end-zone/red-zone targets, target share, snap share) and (b) team scoring environment (Vegas implied team total) as the primary signals, with defensive "TDs/points allowed to position" adding only a small increment; raw trailing TD counts carry negative incremental signal once role is controlled for. Evidence is almost entirely from fantasy-analytics backtests and open-source projects, not peer-reviewed work.

### Cited Findings

Field position and play type (the mechanics behind xTD)
- Carries from the 1-yard line scored 53.9% of the time vs. 3.7% from the 17-yard line (data since 2007); PFF/Clay-style xTD weights each carry by distance and each target by distance plus depth of target using average scoring rates on identical plays — [PFF, Scott Barrett, May 23 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-touchdown-efficiency)
- Share of TDs by distance (seasons since 2010, attributed to Sharp Football Analysis): rushing TDs 86.4% from inside the 20, 73.9% inside the 10, 57.1% inside the 5, 32.5% from the 1; passing TDs 66.8% inside the 20, 43.5% inside the 10, 22.4% inside the 5. 2025 rushing figures: 84.1% / 73.1% / 53.5% / 28.6%. 2025 league average: 2.61 offensive TDs per team per game — [The Lock Talk (Substack), quoting Sharp Football Analysis](https://thelocktalk.substack.com/p/youre-backing-the-wrong-running-back)
- Mike Clay's original oTD (2013) split rushing opportunities into six categories (tailback carry, fullback carry, QB scramble, QB sneak, designed QB run, end-around) because conversion rates differ by type — [PFF archive, "OTD Improvement Project Part 1" (via search snippet; not independently fetched)](https://www.profootballfocus.com/otd-improvement-project-part-1-intro-rushing-analysis/)

Play-level feature set used by the main open-source xTD model (ffopportunity)
- Rushing-play model inputs: `yardline_100, down, ydstogo, qtr, goal_to_go, half_seconds_remaining, game_seconds_remaining, score_differential, fixed_drive, shotgun, no_huddle, qb_dropback, qb_scramble, xpass, run_location, run_gap, run_gap_dir, surface, roof, temp, wind, era, ep, vegas_wp, implied_total, posteam_type, position` (plays filtered to `play_type` in run/qb_kneel) — [ffopportunity ep_preprocess.R](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/main/R/ep_preprocess.R)
- Passing-play model inputs add `air_yards, relative_to_sticks (= air_yards − ydstogo), relative_to_endzone (= air_yards − yardline_100), yards_after_catch, pass_location, qb_hit, total_line, passer_position, receiver_position`; `implied_total` is derived from `total_line`, `spread_line` and `posteam_type`; `era` is a pre/post-2018 flag — [ffopportunity ep_preprocess.R](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/main/R/ep_preprocess.R)

Week-ahead predictive value of usage vs. TD counts (largest self-published sweep found)
- Partial correlations of trailing predictors with next-4-week PPR scoring, controlling for current baseline (NFL 2010–2025, 7,661 player-seasons; a finding had to replicate with the same sign in every season 2021–2025): WR target share +0.217, WOPR +0.192, targets +0.191, snap share +0.167, air-yards share +0.122; WR receiving TDs −0.128, yards/target −0.109. TE target share +0.337, receiving TDs −0.184. RB snap share +0.198, carries +0.125. QB red-zone carry share +0.175, goal-line carry share +0.148; none of 74 QB passing fields replicated as a positive predictor — [GWTTKB, "What Actually Predicts Fantasy Football"](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)
- Same source: red-zone target share predicts WR ceiling at +0.187; summary "the count reverts, but the role that produces it persists" — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)
- Same source (self-described caveat): 272 fields tested, 41 replicated (84.9% failure rate); the author acknowledges ~5% of null signals could pass replication by chance; data and code are not public — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)

Season-to-season predictors of receiving-TD rate (PFF betting research)
- R² of prior-season stat vs. next-season receiving-TD rate (min 50 targets): TD rate 0.058, PFF receiving grade 0.051, total air yards 0.041, % of team air yards 0.033, routes run 0.022, receiving yards 0.016, team passing TDs 0.015, targets 0.007, YAC 0.006; conclusion: receiving TD rate is "nearly random", so project a rate × a volume projection rather than raw TD totals — [PFF, Ben Brown, Jul 27 2021](https://www.pff.com/news/bet-best-bets-receiving-touchdown-player-props-2021)
- Game-level mean receiving-TD outcome for pass-catchers with ≥1 target and ≥20 routes = 0.347; a Poisson simulation "approximates the historical data but is more pessimistic" at the game level — [PFF, Ben Brown, Jul 27 2021](https://www.pff.com/news/bet-best-bets-receiving-touchdown-player-props-2021)

Vegas lines as a feature
- Implied team total = game total ÷ 2 − team spread ÷ 2 (e.g., 3-point favorite in a 50-point game → 26.5) — [Sharp Football Analysis implied-totals tool](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/nfl-implied-team-totals-tool/)
- An open-source anytime-TD simulator sets expected offensive TDs = Vegas implied total × κ (κ measured from closing lines), draws team TD counts from a negative binomial, then allocates TDs to players; it argues the market total already prices weather and QB quality — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Teams beat their implied totals 51–53% of the time; a rising implied total (+2.5 or more) predicted −1.1 PPG for the team's players the next month (n = 6,698 player-months); weekly PPR MAE of market-consensus projections vs. a trailing 5-game average: 4.96 vs. 5.54 overall (n = 6,953), QB 5.79 vs 6.51, RB 5.07 vs 5.67, WR 4.97 vs 5.59, TE 4.23 vs 4.64; early-season consensus correlates r = 0.827 with rest-of-season PPG (2024 and 2025) — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)
- In a 2017–2019 weekly study, "Vegas betting lines" correlated 0.36 with outcomes, versus 0.15–0.20 for offensive points-for averages and <0.10 for points-allowed — [Subvertadown](https://subvertadown.com/article/-fantasy-points-allowed-too-weak-for-making-lineup-decisions)

Defensive matchup data (how much it predicts)
- Fantasy points allowed by position correlated <0.10 with weekly outcomes for most positions (<0.06 for RB/TE/WR); points-for 0.15–0.20; a combined cross-validated model of raw data + points-for + points-allowed reached ~0.35 (2017–2019) — [Subvertadown](https://subvertadown.com/article/-fantasy-points-allowed-too-weak-for-making-lineup-decisions)
- Year-over-year correlation of half-PPR fantasy points allowed (2015–2025): QB 0.27, RB 0.22, TE 0.16, WR "very little"; top-5 defenses repeated top-5 only 20–30% of the time — [4for4, 2026 preseason](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/do-defenses-repeat-fantasy-football-performances)
- Partial r of opponent PPR allowed to position: +0.021 to +0.075 ("small but real"); opponent man-coverage rate, pressure rate, defenders in box: noise; bottom-to-top matchup swing worth about +1.50 PPR (QB), +0.84 (RB), +0.84 (TE), +0.38 (WR); 24,924 player-weeks — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)
- Coverage-specific splits add little: correlation .336 vs .312 when a ≥60% single-high rate is expected — [Fantasy Points, "Why Coverage Matchups Matter" (via search snippet)](https://www.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2025/why-coverage-matchups-matter-in-fantasy)
- Within-player game-script effects: RB +0.99 PPR (+0.64 touches) when favored; WR −0.50; TE +0.57; QB −0.55 — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html)

Regression to the mean of TDs vs. usage
- Of 129 flex-eligible players with >10 TDs who played the next year (past decade as of 2018), only 8.5% increased TDs; average decline ≈5.5 TDs; of the 75 most TD-efficient seasons of the decade, only 8 players scored more the next season — [PFF, Barrett 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-touchdown-efficiency)
- Expected TDs were as predictive of next-season TDs as raw TDs (0.52 vs 0.52) but stickier year-over-year (0.60 vs 0.52) — [PFF, Barrett 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-touchdown-efficiency)
- WRs above their xTD saw an "89.7% decline" in TDs-over-expectation the following season; only 14 of 1,325 WR seasons since 2010 sustained a given efficiency level (model built from nflfastR with down, distance to goal, opportunity type, throw depth/direction; wording of the 89.7% figure is ambiguous) — [The Fantasy Footballers, Marvin Elequin, Jul 12 2023](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/identifying-2023-touchdown-regression-candidates-wrs-fantasy-football/)
- Team level: 72.4% of teams that scored more TDs than their yardage implied scored fewer the next season; of 22 teams since 2010 that outproduced by double digits, only 4 matched the total next year — [Sharp Football Analysis 2020](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/what-we-can-learn-from-team-yardage-to-touchdown-rate-for-2020/) and [2022 follow-up](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/nfl-yards-touchdowns-2022/) (via search snippets)
- Only 14.1% of a team's next-season red-zone opportunity is explained by the previous-year total — [Sharp Football "Red Zone and Team Production" (via search snippet)](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/fantasy-notebook-red-zone-and-team-production/)
- Among 244 WR year-pairs (2010–2017, ≥65 targets, same team), targets were the most stable stat; TDs weak; TD rate and yards/target fluctuate most — [SI, Jul 31 2018](https://www.si.com/nfl/2018/07/31/fantasy-football-2018-most-predictable-wide-receiver-stats)
- A WR's catchable-target rate has averaged only 0.2894 year-over-year correlation since 2023 (an efficiency input to xFP that is unstable) — [PFF 2026 (via search snippet)](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-expected-fantasy-points-wide-receivers-catchable-target-rate-2026)

Modeling-family evidence (season-level and count models)
- Season-level WR TD model (OLS, 1990–2010 train / 2011–2024 test, Pro-Football-Reference data; features: lagged stats, 2-yr rolling averages, age/experience, catch rate, TD per target, target share, red-zone targets/receptions/TDs, team points per game): R² 0.803, MAE 0.82 TDs, RMSE 1.29; CV MAE on train 0.45 ± 0.08; top coefficients catch rate, yards per game, 2-yr avg yards (negative); prior-year TDs (TD_Prev) were not among the top features — [Chung (Brown University undergraduate), Dec 2025, hosted by Wharton Sports Analytics](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CHUNG_Penn-Forecasting-NFL-Wide-Receiver-Touchdowns-with-a-Temporal-Linear-Regression-Model1.pdf)
- Tom Brady's per-game passing TDs fit a Poisson distribution across his career — [Journal of Student Research](https://jsr.org/index.php/path/article/view/1724)
- A Poisson TD-props walkthrough: a mean of 0.86 TDs implies P(0) ≈ 41%; the author notes independence/constant-rate assumptions are violated by two-minute drills and garbage time, suggests negative binomial or ordered logistic, and says the model "is almost certainly too simple to beat market lines" — [Towards Data Science (via search snippets; page returned 403 on fetch)](https://towardsdatascience.com/create-your-own-nfl-touchdown-props-with-python-b3896f19a588)
- A 2014 Stanford CS229 project predicted a QB's exact passing-TD count correctly 38.6% of the time (MSE 1.58) with multinomial logistic regression/SVM, vs. 33% for ESPN that season — [CS229 "Any Given Sunday" (via search snippet)](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2014/Bobak%20Moallemi,%20Matthew%20Wilson,%20Steven%20Hoerning,%20Any%20Given%20Sunday.pdf)
- A commercial first-TD model (PFF First Touchdown Finder, Jan 23 2026) uses: EPA/play on the first 15 offensive snaps (offense, opposing defense, and the differential), touches and touch rate on those plays, adjusted target rate (targets ÷ aimed passes), first-half red-zone carries/targets, opponent red-zone TD rate allowed, and the best available book price; no accuracy claims — [PFF](https://www.pff.com/news/introducing-pff-first-touchdown-finder-a-smarter-way-to-attack-first-td-scorer-markets)
- Features a long-running weekly anytime-TD columnist cites (no formal model): target share, first-read target share, targets per route run, red-zone and end-zone targets, routes/snap share, injuries/inactives, opponent DVOA vs. position and red-zone attempt rates, QB scrambling, game script, weather — [Gilles Gallant, Action Network column syndicated on Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nfl-anytime-touchdown-scorer-props-100024703.html)
- Vegas score weighting example: a 2015 FantasyLabs DFS model weighted "Vegas Score" (percentile of implied totals across seven books) at 50 points vs. 5 points for each of ten other factors; no outcome data given — [FantasyLabs, Sep 24 2015](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/model-building-with-ian-vegas-score/)

### Inferences
- Modeling-approach comparison (synthesis of the above):
  - Play-level GBM/xgboost per-play TD probability (ffopportunity-style) is the most granular and the only approach with a public, versioned implementation; it yields per-play probabilities you can sum into xTD, but it describes opportunity that already happened — a week-ahead forecast still needs a separate model of next week's opportunity (snap/target/goal-line shares × team TD expectation).
  - Rate × volume (PFF) or allocation (popcawn) approaches explicitly separate "how many TDs will the team score" (anchor on implied total; count distribution Poisson or NB) from "who gets them" (shares of goal-line carries, end-zone targets, target share with shrinkage). P(anytime TD) then = 1 − P(0 TDs) under the count distribution, e.g., 1 − e^(−λ) for Poisson.
  - Season-level linear models (Chung) report high R² but are not weekly instruments; the "static" features in that paper (catch rate, yards per game, TD per target) read as same-season statistics, and the paper does not clearly state they are lagged, so the R² of 0.803 should be treated as possibly contaminated by contemporaneous features until the author clarifies. Its cross-validation MAE (0.45) vs test MAE (0.82) gap is also unexplained.
- Feature priority for a weekly anytime-TD model, by evidence strength: (1) goal-line/red-zone opportunity shares and target share (strong, replicated across PFF, GWTTKB, SI), (2) implied team total (used as the anchor in every serious model; consensus projections beat trailing averages by ~10% MAE), (3) snap share/role changes and inactives (large single-week effects, noted everywhere), (4) opponent position-level TD/points allowed (small but non-zero, +0.02 to +0.08 partial r; YoY stability 0.16–0.27), (5) trailing TD counts (negative incremental signal once role is controlled — fade them).
- Count distribution: Poisson is defensible for QB passing TDs (Brady fit), but game-level receiving TDs are described as more dispersed than Poisson at the player level, and the most complete open-source simulator uses a negative binomial at the team level; a developer should test Poisson vs NB via mean/variance check and AIC on their own player-game data rather than assume.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study ranks weekly features for P(anytime TD); the best feature-importance evidence (GWTTKB, PFF) targets fantasy points or season TD rates rather than the binary weekly TD event.
- The ffopportunity vignette exposes feature-importance and partial-dependence plots only as images (with an `xpass` PDP), so the numeric importance ranking of its TD models is not retrievable from text — [vignette](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/articles/rushing_yards_vignette.html).
- I found no direct regression of player TDs on implied team totals with reported coefficients; the implied-total → TD link is asserted (and used as a model anchor) rather than measured in public sources.

## Key Question 2: How is "expected touchdowns" computed in ffopportunity/nflverse, and is there evidence xTD-based projections out-predict actual-TD-based ones?

### Takeaway
ffopportunity computes xTD as the sum of per-play xgboost probabilities (separate rushing and passing TD classifiers, chained after expected-yards/completion models, with Vegas `implied_total` among the inputs); the PFF/ESPN/Clay lineage instead uses empirical scoring rates by yard line and target depth. Evidence that xTD is a better predictor than raw TDs is consistent but thin: equal next-season correlation (0.52 vs 0.52) with greater stability (0.60 vs 0.52), plus multiple regression-rate studies; no evaluation specific to ffopportunity's model was found.

### Cited Findings
- ffopportunity "uses xgboost and tidymodels trained on public nflverse data from 2006-2020"; Expected Fantasy Points are "an estimate of the points an average player would score given a play's situation and opportunity"; `ep_build(season, version)` returns `ep_weekly`, `ep_pbp_pass`, `ep_pbp_rush`, `ep_version`, `timestamp`; weekly output has 159 columns including `pass_touchdown_exp`, `rec_touchdown_exp`, `rush_touchdown_exp` and the matching actuals; R code GPL-3, models and data CC BY-SA 4.0; precomputed data released via GitHub Actions as RDS/parquet/CSV — [ffopportunity site](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/) and [GitHub README](https://github.com/ffverse/ffopportunity)
- `ep_predict()` loads eight xgboost models (`.ubj` files with `.rds` blueprints from a versioned cache folder) and applies them in order — rushing: `rushing_yards → rushing_td → rushing_fd`; passing: `pass_completion → yards_after_catch → pass_touchdown → pass_first_down → passing_int`. Before the passing-TD model it computes `yardline_exp = yardline_100 − air_yards − yards_after_catch_exp`; it overrides `pass_touchdown_exp` with `pass_completion_exp` when `air_yards == yardline_100` (end-zone targets); kneels, spikes and aborted plays are zeroed; two-point attempts route `rushing_td_exp`/`pass_touchdown_exp` into `two_point_conv_exp` — [ffopportunity ep_predict.R](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/main/R/ep_predict.R)
- Model inputs for the TD classifiers are the preprocessed play features listed in KQ1 (including `implied_total`, `total_line`, `vegas_wp`, `ep`, `xpass`, `relative_to_endzone`) — [ep_preprocess.R](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ffverse/ffopportunity/main/R/ep_preprocess.R)
- nflverse loader: `load_ff_opportunity(seasons, stat_type = c("weekly","pbp_pass","pbp_rush"), model_version = c("latest","v1.0.0"))` downloads precomputed data from ffopportunity automated releases; a Python port exists in nflreadpy — [nflreadr reference](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_ff_opportunity.html); [nflreadpy](https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadpy)
- The package site's only methodology article is "Expected Rushing Yards" (Joe Sydlowski, Tan Ho; dated 2025-12-11 in package 0.1.2), consisting of feature-importance, partial-dependence (yards, xpass) and breakdown plots as images; no accuracy or calibration metrics are published — [vignette](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/articles/rushing_yards_vignette.html); [articles index](https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/articles/)
- PFF's xTD (2018): carries weighted by distance from the end zone using average scoring rate on identical carries since 2007; targets weighted by distance and depth of target; sum = TDs "a perfectly average player" would score on the same workload; adapted from Mike Clay's 2013 oTD — [PFF, Barrett 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-touchdown-efficiency)
- ESPN's xTD: "weighs every carry and target to produce one opportunity-based number"; a player with xTD 3.0 "would be expected to score three touchdowns if a league-average player got the same carries and targets from the same areas of the field"; ESPN's xFP replaces passing/rushing TDs with xTD and excludes return TDs — [ESPN 2025 xTD leaderboard (via search snippet; page blocked fetch)](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46168468/2025-fantasy-football-rankings-nfl-expected-td-opportunity-xtd)
- A third-party xTD (Fantasy Footballers, 2023) built from nflfastR assigns each opportunity a probability from 0 to 1 using down, distance to goal, opportunity type, and for targets throw depth and direction; e.g., a deep Week 13 target to Davante Adams carried xTD 0.1649 — [The Fantasy Footballers](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/identifying-2023-touchdown-regression-candidates-wrs-fantasy-football/)
- Evidence xTD ≥ TD as a predictor: next-season TD correlation 0.52 (xTD) vs 0.52 (TD); year-over-year stickiness 0.60 (xTD) vs 0.52 (TD) — [PFF, Barrett 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-touchdown-efficiency); trailing receiving TDs carry negative partial correlation (−0.128 WR, −0.184 TE) with forward scoring while red-zone target share is positive (+0.187) — [GWTTKB](https://www.gwttkb.com/research/what-actually-predicts-fantasy-football.html); WRs above xTD regressed "89.7%" in TDOE the next season — [Fantasy Footballers 2023](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/identifying-2023-touchdown-regression-candidates-wrs-fantasy-football/)
- xFP generally: one model-builder states xFP "is by far the more stable and predictive metric" and becomes more predictive as the season progresses (qualitative) — [Sharp Football Analysis xFP tool (via search snippet)](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/expected-fantasy-points-tool/)

### Inferences
- The two xTD lineages differ materially: ffopportunity is a conditional model (xgboost on ~30 play features including Vegas lines, so a goal-line carry by a 7-point favorite with 2 minutes left is not the same as the same carry in a tie game), whereas PFF/ESPN/Clay xTD is an unconditional empirical lookup by yard line/depth. For betting, the conditional version is closer to "what a book would price," but it was trained on 2006–2020 and has not been publicly validated for 2021+ seasons.
- Because `pass_touchdown_exp` is forced to equal completion probability on end-zone targets, ffopportunity xTD for receivers is highly sensitive to the completion model's calibration on deep/contested targets — a known-unstable input (catchable-target rate YoY r ≈ 0.29 per PFF).
- xTD's advantage is stability rather than point accuracy: use xTD (or opportunity shares) as the prior for next-week scoring rate and shrink realized TD efficiency heavily toward it; the PFF 2018 correlations imply roughly equal one-step predictive power, so the gain is in lower variance of the signal rather than a large accuracy jump.

### Gaps
- No published evaluation (Brier, log loss, calibration, or next-week predictive comparison) of ffopportunity's TD models exists on the package site, README, or vignettes; the xgboost objectives and hyperparameters are not visible in the R source (they live in the serialized `.ubj` models), and the model-training repository I tried (`ffverse/ffopportunity-data`) returned 404.
- Fantasy Points Data's xTD methodology page could not be located (their 2021/2023 xTD regression articles returned 404 or an empty archive); their Data Suite is paid.
- I found no study showing weekly (as opposed to season-level) out-performance of xTD-based projections over actual-TD-based ones with a sportsbook benchmark.

## Key Question 3: What accuracy do published models achieve (Brier/log loss vs base rate), and how do they compare with market-implied probabilities?

### Takeaway
The only public, fully documented anytime-TD backtest against closing prices (2025 season, ~6,000 priced player-games) finds a well-calibrated opportunity model (Brier 0.150 vs 0.164 base rate, log loss 0.475) that is still beaten by the de-vigged median of sportsbook closing prices (Brier 0.140 vs 0.144 in weeks 1–9; 0.127 vs 0.130 in weeks 10–18), and that blending the model into the market does not help; self-reported pick columns claim ~10% ROI but are unaudited.

### Cited Findings
- popcawn/nfl-td-predictor (open source, 2025 season, 10,000-sim Monte Carlo; trained on 2024 plus 2025 weeks before each game): anytime-TD Brier 0.1502 vs 0.1642 base rate (8.5% skill), log loss 0.475, reliability close to the diagonal (predicted 14% → actual 15%; 63% → 63%); sample 6,002 priced player-games — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, market-universe check on scraped ESPN anytime-TD boards: Brier 0.1490 on 1,065 players vs 0.1639 baseline (weeks 1–13, 2025); the book listed 94% of actual scorers — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, versus closing prices (Brier, weeks 1–9 / weeks 10–18): model 0.1440 / 0.1299; other books' raw median 0.1397 / 0.1266; calibrated books 0.1393 / 0.1254; books + 35% model 0.1420 / 0.1283. Quote: "The books are more accurate than the model, on both halves, and mixing the model in doesn't help"; diagnosis: the model spreads TD probability too flatly and underrates featured scorers — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, book calibration (implied chance → actual): 20–30% → 21%; 45–60% → 42%; 60%+ → 59% — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, flat 1u returns at EV > 3%: betting all FanDuel anytime-TD prices −8.7% [−16, −1]; model picks +3.4% [−21, +30] on 1,019 bets while the model claimed ~+45% EV; books' consensus picks −2.0% [−43, +48]; blends +30–40% but driven by a few +4000 longshots in weeks 1–9 and losing in weeks 10–18; conclusion "Nothing showed a proven edge" — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, yardage/reception props: model Brier vs plain season average (wks 1–9 / 10–18) receptions 0.158/0.156 vs 0.170/0.164; receiving yds 0.185/0.177 vs 0.197/0.186; rushing yds 0.173/0.178 vs 0.189/0.185; passing yds 0.218/0.205 vs 0.229/0.234; passing TDs excluded "because they barely beat a season average and their top bucket ran 87% → 76%". Against closing lines the model alone was worse than a coin flip at the line (receiving yds 0.2657/0.2567 vs books 0.2499/0.2503; rushing yds 0.2737/0.2757 vs 0.2506/0.2485; 0.25 = coin flip). Overs hit 46% (rec) and 48% (rush); betting every FanDuel over lost −12.3% (rec) and −8.3% (rush) — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Same project, data limitations: no historical prop prices for direct prop-beating tests; ESPN exposes lines but not prices; blend weights are heuristics not fitted; estimates are "estimate-grade until inactives lock (about 90 minutes before kickoff)" — [popcawn/nfl-td-predictor](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)
- Self-reported pick record (Action Network/Yahoo anytime-TD column, Gilles Gallant): 2025 season 123-269 (31.4%), +44.1 units, 11% ROI; 2026 to date 101-265 (27.5%), +36.4 units, 9.9% ROI; profitable 13 of 18 weeks in each of the last two seasons; typical bets +175 to +450, "sprinkles" at +750 to +2500 sized 0.2u — [Yahoo Sports / Action Network column](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nfl-anytime-touchdown-scorer-props-100024703.html)
- Other partial-season self-reports: Covers' TD column was +24.39 units at Week 13 and "29-plus units" at Week 15 of 2025 — [Covers Week 13](https://www.covers.com/nfl/anytime-touchdown-picks-week-13-2025); [Covers Week 15](https://www.covers.com/nfl/anytime-touchdown-picks-week-15-2025)

- Metric background: a constant base-rate forecast scores Brier p(1−p); log loss punishes confident misses more than Brier — [Ultralytics glossary (via search snippet)](https://www.ultralytics.com/glossary/brier-score); [DRatings](https://www.dratings.com/log-loss-vs-brier-score/)

### Inferences
- Base-rate math: a base-rate Brier of 0.1642 implies the priced-player anytime-TD base rate is about 21% (p(1−p) = 0.164 → p ≈ 0.21); the market's closing-price Brier of ~0.127–0.140 therefore represents roughly 15–23% skill over the base rate, versus ~8.5–12% for the open-source model.
- Benchmark recipe implied by the best available evidence: (1) score your model and the de-vigged median of ≥3 books' closing anytime-TD prices on the same player-games with Brier, log loss and reliability bins; (2) require that a blend "books + w × model" with w fitted on one half of the season improves Brier on the other half — in the one public test, no w > 0 helped; (3) report flat-stake ROI with bootstrap CIs — the ±20–50 point intervals on ~1,000 bets show a single season cannot establish an edge; (4) hold out the last 90 minutes before kickoff, since inactives move both the model and the price.
- The books' bucket calibration in the one public sample (45–60% implied → 42% actual; 60%+ → 59%) hints at slight over-pricing of heavy anytime-TD favorites and roughly fair pricing of 20–30% players, the opposite of a classic favorite-longshot bias — but that is one season, un-devigged by bucket, and should be re-estimated.
- Self-reported tout records (31% hit rate at average odds around +250 to +300 → ~10% ROI) are consistent with either skill or selection/survivorship; without timestamps and closing prices they cannot be audited and should not be used as a benchmark.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or audited backtest of anytime-TD or passing-TD-prop models vs. closing prices exists; the only full-season public one is a single GitHub project (2025 season).
- No public results yet from the OddsShopper "7 AI models vs Kalshi" prop panel — the article shows 0-0 records with grades "to post after this week's games" — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/nfl-week-1-kalshi-ai-prop-panel)
- No published Brier/log loss for passing-TD-total props vs. market; popcawn dropped passing TDs for barely beating a season average.

## Key Question 4: What is known about the efficiency of anytime-TD markets specifically (hold, how fast they move, whether Pinnacle/Circa are the right benchmark)?

### Takeaway
Anytime-TD is a high-hold, high-dispersion, low-limit market: one-way "yes" boards carry 27–47% hold vs ~4% when both sides are offered, about half of players show >5 probability-point gaps across books, and no single sharp book originates prop prices (Pinnacle outsources props and caps them at ~$250–$500), so the usable benchmark is a de-vigged multi-book consensus (or the Kalshi order book) rather than Pinnacle/Circa; closing consensus prices were nonetheless more accurate than the best public model.

### Cited Findings
Hold / vig
- Single-game snapshot (several seasons old): SuperBook implied hold 4.1% when both yes/no sides were posted vs 28.9% when only "yes" was offered; other yes-only books roughly 27–47% — [The Lock Talk, citing Action Network](https://thelocktalk.substack.com/p/youre-backing-the-wrong-running-back); the Action Network comparison (≈1,700 days old) put BetMGM and Borgata at 40.9% hold and noted books treat the market differently, making total hold hard to compute — [Action Network (via search snippet; page returned empty on fetch)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/best-sportsbooks-first-touchdown-anytime-td-props)
- A standard −110/−110 market is 4.8% hold; a penny-wide Kalshi TD contract (54/55) is described as ~103.5% all-in with taker fees (the article's own formula gives closer to 104.5%) — [OddsShopper, Sep 16 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)
- Typical margins cited: ~4.5% for spreads, 6–10% for player props (industry figures, unsourced) — [The Lock Talk](https://thelocktalk.substack.com/p/youre-backing-the-wrong-running-back)
- Player props "often come with heavier juice (−115, −120 or worse)" — [OddsShopper prop-strategy article (via search snippet)](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/sports-betting-prop-strategy-finding-player-prop-inefficiencies-y10)

Price dispersion across books
- Week 1 2026: 1,247 book quotes across 155 anytime-TD props in 14 games (eight books, four exchanges); half of props had a book-to-book gap above 5 implied-probability points; e.g., Justin Jefferson +120 (BetMGM) vs +180 (Bet365) = 9.7 points; Puka Nacua +105 vs +138 (Pinnacle) = 6.8 points; Amon-Ra St. Brown −110 (FanDuel) vs +115 (BetMGM) = 5.9 points — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)
- Week 3 2026 (401 anytime-TD players priced by ≥3 of 8 US books, pulled 2026-09-25): Fanatics alone held the best price on 26.2% of players; adding BetRivers 48.1%, BetMGM 69.1%, DraftKings 82.5%, Hard Rock 93.3%; DraftKings + FanDuel together were best on only 31%; average extra payout from best-of-eight vs Fanatics-only 18.7% — [Steezanomics](https://steezanomics.com/learn/how-many-sportsbooks-do-you-need-for-player-props/)

Who originates prop prices; limits
- "There is no sharpest sportsbook for props"; Pinnacle "doesn't even make their own prop lines" and outsources to Swish Analytics; Pinnacle and Bookmaker limit props to about $250 before gameday and about $500+ on gameday while taking $100,000 on game winners; some large US books now take ~$500 per prop; FanDuel often offers −110 both sides on gameday — [Unabated, Jack Andrews, Jul 31 2026](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props)
- Pinnacle, Betcris and Circa "often post limited prop sheets late in the week" and are not treated as sharp for props; Caesars and FanDuel are the two domestic standouts; FanDuel sets its own prop numbers; MGM and Caesars share an origination source; Caesars allows ≥$500 on sharp accounts and moves aggressively on action — [Establish The Run, Aug 25 2023](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/)
- Props "went from one of the easiest markets to beat to an efficient market"; FanDuel is "more of a source of truth" than some sharper books; FanDuel often posts one-way markets (e.g., +200 to hit a HR with no "no" side), which lets it keep large holds — [BetSmart newsletter, Jul 11 2025](https://betsmart.beehiiv.com/p/efficient-prop-markets)
- Blog-level claims: prop limits often capped at $250–$500 vs $10,000+ for sides; books track CLV to flag sharp accounts (unverified industry assertions) — [betpredictionsite (via search snippet)](https://betpredictionsite.com/blog/why-sportsbooks-limit-prop-bettors/)
- Pinnacle does post anytime-TD prices (it was the best book on Nacua +138 and Kyren Williams −125 in Week 1 2026) — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props); EndzoneAPI's historical anytime-TD archive includes Pinnacle — [EndzoneAPI](https://endzoneapi.com/data/player-props)
- Adjacent evidence (MLB props, vendor study of 1.3M closing lines, 75 books): crossed-market testing ranked Kalshi and ProphetX sharpest, Novig next, DraftKings/FanDuel middle, Bookmaker and Pinnacle softest; main-line Brier gaps between books were small (0.002 RBIs, 0.003 strikeouts, 0.005 hits) — [SmartStake, Jul 7 2026](https://www.smartstake.app/learn/sharpest-sportsbooks-mlb-player-props)

Speed of movement / timing
- Anytime TD is "one particular market that is sure to move plenty" — [CBS Sports Week 3 cheat sheet (via search snippet)](https://www.cbssports.com/betting/news/nfl-week-3-anytime-touchdown-scorer-top-100-cheat-sheet-parlay-jacobs-jeanty-evans-796-parlay); prices fell after publication, e.g., Kenneth Walker to −140 at FanDuel and DraftKings — [RotoWire (via search snippet)](https://www.rotowire.com/news/best-anytime-touchdown-scorer-prop-bets-afc-nfc-championship-games-2026-103207)
- Injury news and tout releases move prop lines; betting after a major move means missing value (no speed quantified) — [Unabated 2026](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props)
- Open-source model treats estimates as provisional until inactives lock ~90 minutes before kickoff — [popcawn](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)

Closing-price accuracy
- De-vigged median of other books' closing anytime-TD prices: Brier 0.1397 (wks 1–9) and 0.1266 (wks 10–18), better than a calibrated opportunity model (0.1440 / 0.1299); blind-betting all FanDuel prices lost 8.7% — [popcawn](https://github.com/popcawn/nfl-td-predictor)

### Inferences
- Hold is dominated by market structure: a two-way yes/no market at ~4% hold vs a yes-only board at ~30–45% hold means "beating the vig" requires either a two-way book/exchange or extreme line-shopping; the Steezanomics numbers imply that holding only DK+FD leaves you with the best price on ~31% of players.
- The right benchmark for a TD model is a de-vigged consensus of several US books' closing prices (what popcawn used) and, since Sept 2025, the Kalshi mid-price, which is penny-wide on most TD contracts — not Pinnacle (outsourced, low-limit) or Circa (limited late-week sheets). The MLB vendor study ranking exchanges sharpest and Pinnacle/Bookmaker softest for props supports this choice, though it is not NFL evidence.
- Market "inefficiency" in anytime TD shows up as cross-book dispersion and timing (pre-inactives) rather than as consensus mispricing: the consensus close was better calibrated than the public model, so any edge must come from (i) shopping the best of 5–8 books, (ii) exchange pricing with fee-adjusted spreads, and (iii) information before inactives/injury news is absorbed.

### Gaps
- No study of closing-line value (CLV) for anytime-TD bets, nor of how long TD lines take to converge after injury news; "fast movement" is asserted in editorial content only.
- No current (2025–2026) measurement of anytime-TD hold by book — the only hold numbers are a single-game snapshot several seasons old.
- No published limits for anytime-TD props at Pinnacle or Circa; the $250/$500 figures are from a tools-vendor article and a 2023 ETR piece.

## Key Question 5: How good are Kalshi/Polymarket NFL prop prices vs sportsbooks (studies, calibration data, fees/spreads), and are there documented inefficiencies?

### Takeaway
For game winners, Kalshi/Polymarket closing prices are statistically indistinguishable from the de-vigged sportsbook consensus (Brier 0.2196 vs 0.2196 on 5,333 games) and Kalshi NBA prices are well calibrated but underreact to news (0.64-for-one); for NFL touchdown props specifically there is no accuracy study — the available evidence is that Kalshi TD contracts (launched 2025-09-04) are penny-wide, dominate Kalshi's NFL prop volume (≈68%), and after fees beat the sportsbook price on 59% of quotes in one week, while Polymarket's player TD markets are thin.

### Cited Findings
Launch and market structure
- Kalshi listed its first NFL player props (first, next, anytime, and multiple-TD scorer contracts) for all Week 1 2025 games, going live 2025-09-04; only the player possessing the ball in the end zone counts (no passing-TD credit); most markets had minimal volume except the opener, where key players' books were ~1¢ wide with thousands of dollars resting; Kalshi self-certified the contracts with the CFTC in August 2025 and offers sports contracts in all 50 states — [InGame, Daniel O'Boyle, Sep 4 2025](https://www.ingame.com/?p=31853)
- Kalshi added NFL passing/rushing/receiving-yards props in early October 2025 — [Next Event Horizon (Substack), Oct 9 2025](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/p/kalshi-launches-nfl-player-prop-bets)
- Kalshi TD-contract rules: rushing and receiving TDs only; defensive/special-teams scores credited to the D/ST unit; canceled games with no make-up resolve "No Touchdown"; NFL.com game book is the resolution source — [simplefunctions.dev market pages (via search snippet)](https://simplefunctions.dev/markets/825fe1f8-9485-4e7d-9954-2562f3f5ba17)
- Polymarket runs anytime and first-TD boards (every player in a preseason slate; two boards for Steelers–Browns); archived 2025 player TD markets show volumes of $10 to $515; rules count rushing/receiving/special-teams TDs, not passing; defensive TDs credited to the unit — [Action Network (via search snippet)](https://www.actionnetwork.com/news/steelers-vs-browns-anytime-td-scorer-predictions-best-nfl-td-props-today-on-polymarket-tnf-best-bets); [Polyguana archive (via search snippet)](https://polyguana.com/market/943084)

Kalshi NFL prop liquidity and pricing vs books (Sept 2026)
- Kalshi NFL prop volume shares: touchdowns scored 43.2%, first touchdown 25.0% (≈68% combined), receiving yards 12.3%, passing TDs 7.4%, passing yards 5.3%, rushing yards 3.5%, receptions 2.3%; median bid-ask spreads: TD and first-TD 1¢, passing TDs 5.5¢, receiving yards 8¢, rushing yards 5¢; volume-weighted TD spread 1.16¢; 53% of Thursday TD contracts penny-wide — [OddsShopper, Sep 16 2026](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)
- Example depth (Lions at Bills, 2:15 pm ET 2026-09-16): Jahmyr Gibbs 1+ TD 68/69 (−239 effective after fee, 18,629 contracts); James Cook 54/55 (40,247); Josh Allen 48/49 (39,157); Jared Goff 4/5 (82,317); all quoted 1¢ wide — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)
- Week 1 2026 comparison (fee-adjusted): Kalshi was the better price on 739 of 1,247 quotes (59%), the single best number on ~10% and the worst on ~1%; e.g., St. Brown Kalshi +101 vs FanDuel −110 / BetMGM +115; Jefferson Kalshi +173 vs BetMGM +120 / Bet365 +180; maker vs taker: St. Brown taker 43¢ (+124) vs maker 42¢ (+138) — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props) (vendor content; one week of data)
- Single-game example (Rams–49ers, 2026-09-10): Nacua 40¢ anytime cost $41.69 per $100 payout with fees vs $43.48 at DraftKings; the sims-vs-Kalshi comparison used "¢ per projected point" rather than probabilities and reported no track record — [Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/prediction-markets/rams-vs-49ers-first-touchdown-sims-vs-kalshi)
- One vendor claims Kalshi prices "often diverge from implied sportsbook probabilities by 3–7pp, especially on road underdogs in divisional games" — no methodology or sample given — [PropsBot (via search snippet)](https://propsbot.ai/kalshi-sports-player-props/)
- A comparison board benchmarks Kalshi/Polymarket prop prices against a de-vigged median of sportsbook lines and notes most props are not priced until a day or two before kickoff — [predictionmarketspicks.com (via search snippet)](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/nfl/props)

Fees
- Kalshi fee schedule effective 2026-07-07: taker fee = 0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up; fees charged only on orders that match immediately; maker fees apply only on specifically listed markets (several listed sports series carry maker/taker multipliers of 1); canceling a resting order is free — [Kalshi fee schedule PDF (via search snippet)](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf); [Kalshi help "Fees"](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823805-fees)
- Worked taker fees per contract: 50¢ → 1.75¢ (3.5% of cash in); 25¢ → ≈1.31¢ (5.3%); 16¢ → ≈0.94¢ (5.9%); 90¢ → 0.63¢ (0.7%); a single 56¢ contract rounds up to a flat 2¢ (3.6%); crossing a 5¢ spread costs ~2.5¢ each way vs mid; DFS GPP rake ≈10–15% of entries, roughly 4× the Kalshi toll on the same stake — [Stokastic, Aug 12 2026](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-fees-vs-dfs-rake)
- Third-party sources give the Kalshi maker formula as ⌈0.0175 × C × P × (1 − P)⌉ where maker fees apply (unverified against the official schedule) — [rivermarkets (via search snippet)](https://www.rivermarkets.com/insights/kalshi-fees.html)
- Polymarket: taker-only fees, fee = C × rate × p × (1 − p); Sports rate 0.05 (peak $1.25 per 100 shares at 50¢), makers pay nothing, 15% maker rebate on sports — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees); sports fees began 2026-02-18 starting with NCAA basketball and Serie A, expanding to all sports — [Pine Analytics (via search snippet)](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)

Accuracy / calibration research (none prop-specific)
- Game winners, six leagues, 5,333 games (May 2025–Jul 2026): Brier Kalshi 0.2196, Polymarket Global 0.2198, equal-weighted 11-book de-vigged sportsbook consensus 0.2196; home-side calibration slopes 0.956 / 0.954 / 1.002; all pairwise Brier differences within ±0.001 (90% CI); Pinnacle-as-benchmark robustness check; no player props; undergraduate project "in final review" — [Calibrating the Crowd (Northwestern undergraduate research, GitHub README)](https://github.com/dylanmryan/calibrating-the-crowd)
- Kalshi NBA winner contracts, 2025-04-15 to 2026-05-25, 1,438 games, 409,512 contract-minutes: mean Brier 0.204 at 24h before tipoff → 0.199 at close (improvement 0.0046, SE 0.0013); MAE 0.406 → 0.397; calibration slopes 0.977 (24h) and 0.964 (close) with intercepts near zero; a 10-point pre-game revision is associated with ~13 points of realized probability; in-game, a one-minute change in a benchmark probability moves the Kalshi midpoint only 0.64-for-one, with the remainder predicting drift over the following minutes, worse when liquidity is low — [Angelini & De Angelis, arXiv:2606.07811, Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811)
- Kalshi NBA/MLB/NHL moneylines (~23M trades, Mar–May 2026): calibration parameters near 1 from 30 to 240 minutes before expiry but step-like in the final 10 minutes (Platt slope 1.62 NBA, 2.05 MLB, 4.56 NHL in the 5-minute bucket); cross-game parlays priced above the product of legs with ~3% median inflation per added leg (median R: 2 legs 0.991, 5 legs 1.005, 8 legs 1.066, 11 legs 1.305); parlay calibration curves 2–10 points below the diagonal (a 0.30 parlay wins ~24%) — [Moshrefi, arXiv:2607.14430, Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14430v1)
- Kalshi Research (Kagan & Baiocchi, Aug 2026): 2,243,741 resolved markets 2021–mid-2026; Brier ≈0.08–0.09 at a 3-month horizon → ≈0.02 at close; naive accuracy 88.3% → 97.2%; calibration improves with volume and trader count; sports, mentions and intraday markets are excluded from the "cleaner" calibration subsample because information arrives rapidly; all data except trader count is public via the API — [Kalshi Research calibration page](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/calibration); [Kalshi News summary, Aug 20 2026](https://news.kalshi.com/p/prediction-market-calibration-how-accurate-is-kalshi)
- UCD/GWU working paper "Makers and Takers" (Bürgi, Deng, Whelan): Kalshi prices become more accurate approaching close but show a favorite–longshot bias (all categories) — [UCD WP2025_19 (via search snippet; PDF fetch failed)](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); [GWU version](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)
- Vanderbilt study (Clinton & Huang) of ~2,500 political markets (~$2.5B volume) in the final five weeks of the 2024 election: "accuracy" Polymarket 67%, Kalshi 78%, PredictIt 93%; 58% of Polymarket presidential markets showed negative serial correlation; Kalshi responded that calibration is the proper test and that by that measure it is "almost perfectly accurate" — [DL News, Dec 5 2025](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/markets/polymarket-kalshi-prediction-markets-not-so-reliable-says-study)
- Third-party calibration tracker across Kalshi, Manifold, Metaculus and Polymarket — [Calibration City (via search snippet)](https://manifund.org/projects/calibration-city)

### Inferences
- Fee-adjusted cost comparison: a penny-wide Kalshi TD market costs roughly 3.5–4.5% all-in for a taker (1¢ spread + ~1.75¢ fee at mid-prices), i.e., similar to a two-way −110 sportsbook market and far below a yes-only sportsbook board (27–47% hold); resting (maker) orders on TD contracts pay no fee on most sports markets, which is the cheapest execution available for a model-driven bettor.
- Polymarket's sports taker rate (0.05) is lower than Kalshi's (0.07), but its NFL player-TD markets are too thin (hundreds of dollars) to be either a benchmark or an execution venue; Kalshi's TD books (tens of thousands of contracts on starters) are deep enough to serve as a benchmark but mostly only within a day or two of kickoff.
- Documented inefficiencies on exchanges are (i) parlay markup (~3%/leg on Kalshi), (ii) underreaction to live information (0.64-for-one) and step-like calibration in the final minutes, and (iii) early-week thinness; there is no documented systematic mispricing of pre-game TD contracts vs sportsbooks — the 59%-better-price result is a cross-venue dispersion finding (useful for line-shopping), not an accuracy finding.

### Gaps
- No study compares Kalshi (or Polymarket) NFL player-prop or anytime-TD closing prices with sportsbook closes on Brier/log loss; the only exchange-vs-book accuracy work is on game winners and moneylines.
- Kalshi's official fee page could not be fetched (HTTP 429); the formula is corroborated by the schedule-PDF snippet and two secondary sources, but sport-specific multipliers for NFL prop series were not verified.
- Kalshi's handling of a player who is inactive (whether the contract resolves "No" or is voided) was not confirmed from an official rules page; one third-party guide's description was internally inconsistent and is omitted.
- Polymarket's NFL player-prop fee status (whether NFL markets are in the "Sports" category rollout) is not confirmed in official docs.

### Inferences
- A practical free-ish backtest stack for anytime TD and passing-TD props: nflverse pbp + `load_ff_opportunity` for features and xTD; nflverse `load_schedules` for spreads/totals (treat as closing-ish but verify against another source since provenance is undocumented); EndzoneAPI closing/opening prop lines for 2023–2025 (joinable on `gsis_id`) within the free credit budget for ~50 games/month or $19/month for a season; Kalshi historical trades/candlesticks for exchange prices from Sept 2025 onward (anytime, first, next, multiple TD markets). This covers three seasons of sportsbook closes and one-plus season of exchange closes.
- Because EndzoneAPI's 2023–2025 "closing" line is the last snapshot 1–5 hours before kickoff, it will usually predate inactives (released 90 minutes pre-kick); a model evaluated against it is not being tested against the true close — use The Odds API 5-minute snapshots or Kalshi trades near kickoff when strict closing-line tests matter.

### Gaps
- No Kaggle or other bulk, free, multi-season archive of anytime-TD closing prices was found.
- The provenance (sportsbook/aggregator) and timing (open vs close) of nflverse `spread_line`/`total_line` are not documented in nflreadr's dictionary or nfldata's DATASETS.md.
- The Odds API's current plan pricing for historical props was not confirmed from its own site (only a competitor's listing).
